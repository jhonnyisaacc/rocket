# Memecoin research — live pass 02

Captured 2026-09-15 after the initial Fomo/X/PONS pass. Research only. The workflow remains `NO_EDGE_VALIDATED`.

## Main result of this pass

The research problem should be split into two models, in this order:

1. **Risk gate:** identify launches where the apparent early demand is likely to be insider-controlled, bundled, wash-generated, copycat-driven, or likely to dump soon after migration.
2. **Conditional continuation model:** only inside the surviving set, test whether independent liquidity acceleration, buyer breadth, social timing, and persistent trader-cohort participation predict *costed* forward returns.

This ordering matters. Graduation, volume, and large account PnL are not equivalent to tradable alpha. A model that predicts graduation but admits manipulated launches can select better exit liquidity rather than better entries.

## New literature evidence

### MELT / MemeTrans: bundled ownership is a first-class feature

The public MELT repository and paper provide the closest reusable template for our Solana work. The dataset contains 41,470 launches that completed creation, launchpad sale, migration, and DEX trading, with more than 200M transactions. It exposes 122 features across context, trading activity, holder concentration, and time-series behavior, plus bundle traces intended to link accounts controlled by one entity.

The most important result is not the model score; it is the ownership correction. The authors report that 36.5% of supply that appears to be held by independent accounts is controlled by coordinated accounts after bundle identification. High-risk launches show larger early accumulation, fewer holders and buy transactions, and larger average buys. The top-10 and top-20 concentration measures become materially higher after bundled accounts are grouped.

The practical implication is that raw holder count and raw top-10 share are insufficient. Our dataset needs both observed and bundle-adjusted concentration, with the method and confidence recorded. [MELT paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13480), [public code and data instructions](https://github.com/git-disl/MELT).

MELT is especially useful because it is reproducible: the authors publish pre-migration transactions, bundle traces, an optional post-migration set, feature generation code, and chronological training/evaluation code. The repository is non-commercially licensed, so it is a research reference rather than an asset to copy into a commercial product without checking terms.

### Five-minute rug-risk detection is a realistic first horizon

A newer Solana study assembles 6.4M tokens over seven months and reports that many rug-like outcomes appear within one hour. It evaluates classic models, especially gradient boosting, using only the first five minutes of trading, and finds that combining PumpFun and Raydium data helps with cross-platform regime shift.

This supports a practical sequence for us: first collect a five-minute feature snapshot, then evaluate 15m/1h/6h/24h outcomes. We should not pretend that a five-minute risk model proves a profitable strategy; its first job is to reduce catastrophic selection error. [Catching the Rug](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20271).

### The launchpad sale creates a structural insider advantage

MELT describes the launchpad bonding-curve mechanics as a price ladder where earlier buyers pay lower prices. In its labeled sample, 73% of launches fell below 40% of migration price within 20 minutes, and 37.5% of the ambiguous high-price-path cases were manually labeled manipulated. The authors also report that 84.13% of their default labels were high-risk; that percentage is sample- and label-definition-specific, not a universal base rate.

This changes the research target: the default should be **avoidance and survival**, not “find the next 100x.” If a continuation rule only works by entering before the market can observe the insider distribution, it is not a robust public-data strategy.

## Feature specification v0.2

Every feature must carry `event_time`, `available_at`, `decision_time`, source, and confidence. A feature is unusable if it was only available after the decision timestamp.

### Identity and lifecycle

- `chain_id`, `contract_address` / Solana mint, launchpad, factory/program version, creator, pool/DEX, launch timestamp, migration timestamp;
- `identity_join_method` and `identity_confidence`; ticker/name joins are never sufficient;
- lifecycle state: bonding curve, migrated, DEX live, liquidity warning, trading halted or stale.

### Observed market behavior

- liquidity, market cap, volume, unique buyers/sellers, buy/sell count and notional imbalance;
- buy-size distribution, inter-arrival times, trade concentration, price impact, realized volatility;
- holder count and top-10/top-20 concentration both raw and adjusted for identified bundles;
- first-five-minute and first-thirty-minute slopes, acceleration, and persistence rather than a single endpoint.

### Manipulation and ownership controls

- `wt1_atomic_self_trade_count`, `wt1_volume_share`, and same-price/same-window wash flags;
- `creator_cluster_id`, funding-hop depth, cluster launch count, cluster share of supply, and cluster historical outcomes;
- `bundle_adjusted_top10`, `bundle_adjusted_top20`, and the number of high-confidence linked accounts;
- coordinated-sell flags: atomic multi-sender dump, sender count, one-hour and 24-hour transfer-to-dumper windows;
- copycat similarity at name/symbol/image/description levels, `is_original`, and original age at decision;
- direct-contract or bot-like share, clearly labeled as a sophistication/automation proxy rather than proof of fraud.

### Participation quality

- unique non-cohort buyers at 5m/15m/30m;
- SOL/base-asset inflow from non-linked accounts;
- fraction of buyers who remain holders after 15m/1h/6h;
- overlap with pre-registered trader cohorts, but exclude cohort wallets from the independent-participation count;
- capacity estimate: expected slippage for fixed notional and liquidity-adjusted volume.

### Social and narrative timing

- exact public-post URL, author identity, verification status, post timestamp, token-address match, and post type;
- `post_to_launch_seconds` and `post_to_observation_seconds`;
- whether the post is original, quoted, recycled, or after the price move;
- independent-source count and engagement velocity, without treating engagement as independent demand.

### Outcomes

For each eligible snapshot, record mid-price and executable-price forward returns at 5m, 15m, 1h, 6h, 24h, and 7d, net of fees, estimated slippage, and a capacity cap. Also record maximum adverse excursion, maximum favorable excursion, time to +20% / -20%, liquidity impairment, and whether the token became unexecutable or stale.

## Revised hypotheses

### H1 — Risk gate has value before continuation

Among all eligible launches, a gate based on bundle-adjusted concentration, creator lineage, early accumulation, buyer breadth, and coordinated-sell risk should reduce the left tail and improve survival. Success is measured by avoided loss and calibration, not by the number of graduates selected.

### H2 — Independent breadth matters only after concentration controls

Raw unique buyers may be a disguised count of coordinated accounts. The useful predictor is independent buyer breadth and base-asset inflow after known creator, funding, bundle, and cohort links are removed. Compare raw versus adjusted features directly.

### H3 — Automation has two meanings

Automated/direct-contract activity can be a manipulation warning, but sophisticated or bot-like traders may also provide liquidity and increase graduation probability. Therefore model `graduation_probability` and `costed_forward_return` as separate targets, and report both. Never use `isBot` as a one-dimensional veto without validation.

### H4 — Social posts are attention shocks, not trade confirmations

An X post becomes researchable only when the token address is unambiguous, the post precedes the observation, and an independent on-chain response follows. A public trader’s reputation is a cohort-selection variable, not proof that the next post is profitable.

### H5 — Consistency is robust across time and outliers

For Fomo candidates and any discovered wallet cohort, report equal-weighted results by token and wallet, then repeat after removing the top one and top three outcomes. Require positive results in disjoint time blocks, a bounded drawdown, and performance that is not explained by a single ecosystem or one marked-to-market position.

## Immediate experiment design

The first implementable experiment is a defensive “survival panel”:

1. Build a timestamped panel of launches from PumpFun/Solana and the PONS ecosystem, with a fixed five-minute decision point.
2. Reproduce as many MELT-style features as data access permits, especially bundle-adjusted concentration, early accumulation, number of holders, and time-to-migration.
3. Add the wash/coordinated-sell/copycat detectors from the larger pump.fun study.
4. Exclude or separately stratify launches with missing price, stale liquidity, unlocked-liquidity warnings, ambiguous identity, or post-decision metadata.
5. Use chronological train/validation/test blocks and a no-trade baseline. Score AUPRC/calibration for risk, then costed forward-return distributions for the survivors.
6. Only after the risk gate is stable, test the incremental value of Fomo cohort overlap and X timing.

The pass/fail rule is intentionally strict: no promotion unless the result survives a time split, realistic costs/capacity, top-outlier removal, and a negative-control comparison against random eligible launches.

## What this means for PONS and the current case studies

PONS remains an ecosystem-mechanics case study, not a validated signal. Its launchpad/factory version, fee flow, migration, locked-liquidity behavior, and independent-participant breadth should be measured as a panel. The PONS token and PONS-launched tokens must be separate populations.

SCRIBE remains a useful live warning case: its observed extreme return coexisted with very high volume relative to liquidity and an unlocked-liquidity warning. It should be retained as an adverse-selection example, not as evidence of a buy setup.

The Fomo overlap cohort remains useful for longitudinal labeling. It must be joined to actual token-level timestamps and evaluated on realized plus marked-to-market results, with account-level PnL concentration and look-ahead limitations reported.

## New operational inference

The most promising near-term research product is a **risk-adjusted discovery board** with four separate columns:

- `attention`: X/Fomo visibility and narrative velocity;
- `participation`: adjusted independent buyers and base-asset inflow;
- `integrity`: bundles, creator lineage, wash/coordinated-sell/copycat risk;
- `survival`: costed forward return and drawdown after each snapshot.

An item can be high-attention and high-participation but fail integrity; it can pass integrity and still fail survival. The board should preserve all four values instead of compressing them into one “alpha score.”

## Current conclusion

There may be a researchable edge in **avoiding manipulated launches and selecting for independent participation**, but the evidence does not yet establish a positive-return trading strategy. The next valuable artifact is a point-in-time feature table and a survival/risk backtest, not a signal leaderboard.

## Target product contract

The eventual product is an alerting system, not an autonomous trader:

> When a live monitor observes a specific asset and a fully defined sequence of point-in-time conditions, emit a buy-candidate notification for a specific timestamp and execution context.

The notification should contain:

- exact chain, contract/mint, pool, and launchpad identity;
- trigger timestamp and the latest eligible snapshot;
- trigger state: integrity gate, independent-participation measures, liquidity/capacity, social/cohort evidence, and market regime;
- suggested observation range rather than an unconditional buy instruction;
- invalidation conditions, maximum acceptable age, liquidity floor, and stale-data timeout;
- evidence links and the features that were available before the trigger;
- later labels: executable return, slippage, maximum adverse excursion, and whether the signal would have survived 5m/15m/1h/6h/24h.

The live engine should be stateful. A token moves through `DISCOVERED → ELIGIBILITY_CHECK → WATCHING → TRIGGERED → EXPIRED`, with a separate `BLOCKED` state for manipulation, identity, liquidity, or data-quality failures. Repeated alerts for the same token must be deduplicated and linked to the original state transition.

The signal should require a sequence, not one threshold. A candidate example is:

1. identity and lifecycle are confirmed;
2. liquidity is executable and safety/integrity gates pass;
3. independent buyers and base-asset inflow accelerate across consecutive windows;
4. price holds above the post-event reference without a coordinated-sell or concentration breach;
5. any X/Fomo evidence is timestamped before the trigger and is treated as supporting context only;
6. the signal expires quickly if the state changes or the data becomes stale.

The research pass/fail target therefore becomes **alert quality**, not theoretical coin selection: precision at a fixed alert budget, costed forward-return distribution, false-alert rate, time-to-invalidation, capacity, and stability across time blocks and launchpads. No live notification should be promoted until it beats a no-alert baseline after costs and survives outlier, clan, ecosystem, and regime controls.
