## Research evidence that changes the design

1. The September 2026 pump.fun study analyzes 15.2M launches and identifies wash trading, creator-address obfuscation, coordinated sells, copycats, and social-media manipulation. It reports a conservative lower bound of millions of wash-trading transactions and finds that manipulation can materially increase apparent graduation probability. This makes manipulation detection a prerequisite, not an optional feature. [Szwajcok et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.10246v1)

2. A cross-chain meme-token study reports artificial-growth evidence in 82.8% of tokens with returns above 100%, including wash trading and liquidity-pool-based price inflation. This is a warning about conditioning on winners: the apparent “successful” sample may be the most manipulated sample. [Mongardini and Mei, 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.01963)

3. The pump.fun prediction study finds that fast liquidity accumulation is more informative than raw trade count, while bot activity is harmful to naïve prediction. It does not establish a profitable price-only strategy. [Predicting the success of new crypto-tokens](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.14860)

4. The coordinated-sniper study finds persistent wallet rings, but shows why naïve early-wallet copying is not causal evidence; results weaken after contamination adjustments. Wallets must be evaluated as cohorts with independent validation and no look-ahead. [Coordinated Sniper Cohorts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02795)

5. An event-study of Twitter promotion and crypto pump-and-dumps finds pre-event abnormal returns and delayed selling by Twitter-dependent investors. Social activity is therefore best treated as a timing/attention variable with a likely adverse-selection penalty, not as a bullish label. [Ardia and Bluteau](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4703467)

## PONS theory to test, not assume

PONS is a useful ecosystem case study because the launchpad mechanics are explicit. Official documentation says Robinhood Chain uses chain ID 4663, launches trade against WETH, and PONS V2 uses a bonding curve before graduation to a locked Uniswap V4 pool. The official repository documents a fee/buyback architecture, while the docs explicitly warn that buybacks and burns do not guarantee a higher token price.

The testable idea is not “buy PONS because it burns.” It is:

> Does growth in launchpad activity, creator participation, real fee flow, and post-graduation retention create measurable, out-of-sample lead-lag information for the ecosystem token or newly launched tokens?

Required controls are launchpad regime, factory version, chain activity, liquidity, creator funding clusters, wash-trading flags, and market-wide ETH/SOL risk. PONS itself must be joined by exact chain and contract address, because copied names are common.

Sources: [official PONS docs](https://docs.ponsfamily.com/), [PONS V2 docs](https://docs.ponsfamily.com/v2), [verified contract repository](https://github.com/ponsdotdev/ponsfamily).

## Current hypotheses

### H1 — Clean launch continuation

Among new launches, acceleration in *real* liquidity and unique-buyer breadth may predict 5m/15m/1h continuation after costs, but only after excluding atomic self-trades, creator-linked wallets, coordinated sells, copycats, and unlocked-liquidity hazards.

### H2 — Ecosystem reflexivity

For PONS-like launchpads, launch count alone is probably noise. A stronger candidate is the interaction of real launch volume, fee generation, graduate retention, and breadth of independent participants. Test both the launchpad token and new launches, with regime and activity controls.

### H3 — Social-to-chain lead-lag

A public X post is informative only if its timestamp precedes a measurable, independent on-chain response and the author has a verified historical record. Posts after entry, recycled promotions, copycat accounts, and ambiguous tickers are rejected or separately labeled.

### H4 — Consistent trader cohort

The useful object is not a “best wallet.” It is a cohort whose net, costed performance remains positive across disjoint time blocks and whose results do not depend on one outlier. Score wallets on realized plus marked-to-market return, hit rate, median return, drawdown, average hold, turnover, liquidity capacity, and concentration-adjusted PnL.

## Minimum dataset for the next three-day study

Each point-in-time observation should include:

- exact chain, contract, launchpad/factory, creator, and launch timestamp;
- liquidity, market cap, volume, unique buyers/sellers, buy/sell imbalance, and holder concentration;
- creator funding lineage, wallet-cohort overlap, atomic buy/sell and same-block behavior, coordinated-sell flags, and copycat similarity;
- public-X source, author identity, post timestamp, post type, and whether it is before or after the observed entry;
- outcomes at 5m, 15m, 1h, 6h, 24h, and 7d, net of fees, slippage, and liquidity capacity.

The three daily passes should be:

1. **Discovery:** collect new launches, PONS ecosystem activity, Fomo participants, and candidate X events.
2. **Adversarial review:** reject bad identity joins, manipulation, hindsight, social duplication, and outlier-dependent trader claims.
3. **Evaluation:** append forward outcomes, update cohort statistics, and promote a hypothesis only when it survives a time split and realistic costs.
