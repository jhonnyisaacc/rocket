# Verdict (plain English)

We tested whether raising cash before selloffs and buying back near the lows can beat simply holding all eleven stocks equally. In the twenty months used for testing, it could: similar return per unit of risk with a much shallower worst fall (about minus seventeen percent versus minus twenty-eight). But on the final four months, which the rules never saw during testing, the strategy lost on both counts: less return per unit of risk and a deeper fall (minus eighteen versus minus thirteen percent). After forty-three variations, the statistical check says the return edge is indistinguishable from luck; only the smaller test-window drawdowns look structural, and even those failed in July 2026, when the strategy bought the June rebound and rode the next leg down. The plain recommendation is to hold the eleven stocks equally and rebalance occasionally: it is simpler, cheaper, and won the only fair out-of-sample test. If you still want crash timing, use v6 only as a cushion with eyes open: it protects when it sits in cash early into a fall and gets whipsawed when a rally fails right after it buys.
