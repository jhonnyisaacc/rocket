# Bakeoff universe

Phase 1 only. This file freezes which published rules are candidates. It does not rank them, does not run a replay, and does not change `rocket crypto scan`. `execution_enabled` stays false. Nothing here is the live detector.

Clocks are constraints, not theories. Bias may use a completed week or day. The setup bar is a completed 4h bar. The trigger is a completed 1h bar. The fill is the next 1h open after that 1h close. A fill on a daily or weekly bar is not allowed. A rule whose own clock is monthly or weekly can use the 4h bar only as the moment a higher-timeframe fact becomes tradable. Adding a 4h price pattern the source does not state is a different strategy and is not done.

"Can sit on 4h/1h" means the source's prediction is still the prediction after the fill is moved to the next 1h open. It does not mean the source tested 4h bars.

Sources below are papers or repo files that state a rule. Marketing pages with no rule are omitted. Ugly repo rules are kept and labeled.

## 1. Time-series momentum / trend

### Moskowitz, Ooi, Pedersen (2012), time-series momentum

Source: Tobias J. Moskowitz, Yao Hua Ooi, Lasse Heje Pedersen, "Time series momentum," Journal of Financial Economics 104 (2012) 228–250. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2011.11.003. Working text: https://w4.stern.nyu.edu/facdir/lpederse/papers/TimeSeriesMomentum.pdf

What it predicts: the sign of an instrument's own past excess return predicts its next return, for lookbacks up to about 12 months, then partial reversal. The strategy they study in depth is 12-month lookback, 1-month hold.

Quoted rule (section 4 and Figure 2 caption):

> "For each instrument in every month, the trend strategy goes long (short) the contract if the excess return over the past 12 months of being long the instrument is positive (negative), and scales the size of the bet to be inversely proportional to the ex ante volatility of the instrument."

> "we focus on the properties of the 12-month time series momentum strategy with a 1-month holding period (e.g., k = 12 and h = 1), which we refer to simply as TSMOM."

> "We size each position (long or short) so that it has an ex ante annualized volatility of 40%. That is, the position size is chosen to be 40%/σ_{t−1}."

Native timeframe: monthly decision, monthly hold, on liquid futures (their sample is 58 instruments from 1985, not crypto perps).

4h/1h: the sign and the 40% vol scale cannot be turned into a 4h price setup without lying. A 4h bar can only timestamp the already-closed month. That dummy bar is not a setup. No contract in `CONTRACTS.md` pretends TSMOM is a 4h breakout. The vol scale belongs to family 5 as position size, not as a separate entry.

Failure modes: the paper's futures are exchange-traded with rolls; crypto perps have funding, which their excess return does not include. Their ex-ante vol is a monthly estimate on those contracts. Applying 12-month sign to a 4h return, or replacing 40% with another target after seeing perp PnL, is a different strategy. Costs in their main factor are not Hyperliquid fees.

### Brock, Lakonishok, LeBaron (1992), trading-range break and moving average

Source: William Brock, Josef Lakonishok, Blake LeBaron, "Simple Technical Trading Rules and the Stochastic Properties of Stock Returns," Journal of Finance 47 (1992) 1731–1764. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1992.tb04681.x. Text used: http://technicalanalysis.org.uk/support-and-resistance/BrockLakonishokLeBaron1992.pdf

What it predicts: a daily price that breaks a local high or low, or a short moving average that crosses a long one, has a different subsequent return than the unconditional daily return. Tested on the Dow Jones index, 1897–1986. Not a crypto claim.

Quoted rules:

> "buy and sell signals are generated by two moving averages, a long period, and a short period. In the second method signals are generated as stock prices hit new highs and lows."

> "A buy signal is generated when the price penetrates the resistance level. The resistance level is defined as the local maximum. ... a sell signal is generated when the price penetrates the support level which is the local minimum price."

> "Maximum (or minimum) prices were determined based on the past 50, 150, and 200 days. In addition, the rule is implemented with and without a one percent band. ... For the trading range break rule we compute 10-day holding period returns following buy and sell signals."

Native timeframe: one index price per day. Fixed 10-day forward window after a signal. The moving-average lengths they report are the short/long pairs (1, 50), (1, 150), (5, 150), (1, 200), (2, 200), with and without a 1% band. Those pairs are not re-selected here.

4h/1h: the trading-range break can sit without a new pattern. The channel is the max and min of the last 50 completed daily closes. The setup is the first completed 4h close that penetrates that channel. The fill is the next 1h open. The 10-day hold stays 10 UTC days, not 10 four-hour bars. Using 50 four-hour bars, or an EMA length chosen for perp PnL, would be a different rule. The moving-average rule is the same family and is not given its own contract, so this bakeoff does not run a length horse-race.

Failure modes: the Dow series is a survivor index and a cash index, so there is no funding and no perp liquidation. Their statistic averages overlapping 10-day windows; it is not a single netted account. Shorts on the historical Dow are not the same as short perps. The 1% band and the 150- and 200-day channels are in the paper and are not in the frozen contract. Leaving them out is to avoid a search, not because a table picked 50.

Contract: `trb-50d` in `CONTRACTS.md`.

## 2. Cross-sectional momentum on a point-in-time liquid-perp universe

### Jegadeesh and Titman (1993)

Source: Narasimhan Jegadeesh and Sheridan Titman, "Returns to Buying Winners and Selling Losers: Implications for Stock Market Efficiency," Journal of Finance 48 (1993) 65–91. https://www.bauer.uh.edu/rsusmel/phd/jegadeesh-titman93.pdf

What it predicts: NYSE/AMEX stocks with high past returns beat stocks with low past returns over the next 3 to 12 months.

Quoted construction:

> "At the beginning of each month t the securities are ranked in ascending order on the basis of their returns in the past J months. Based on these rankings, ten decile portfolios are formed that equally weight the stocks contained in the top decile, the second decile, and so on. ... In each month t, the strategy buys the winner portfolio and sells the loser portfolio, holding this position for K months."

J and K are each 3, 6, 9, or 12 months. A second set skips one week between formation and holding. Later papers often skip a month. This paper's own skip is one week.

Native timeframe: monthly ranks, monthly overlapping holds, equity deciles.

4h/1h: the rank is monthly. A 4h price filter is not in the paper. Deciles of a few dozen liquid perps are not NYSE deciles. Equal weight and a one-week skip can be clock-mapped onto a 1h fill after month-end, but that mapping still predicts a multi-month equity relative return, and the book is too small for ten deciles to mean what they meant in 1993. Not contracted. The crypto weekly sort below is the family member that matches a perp cross-section.

Failure modes: bid-ask bounce (why they skip a week), equal-weight small stocks, no perp funding, overlapping portfolios that share names, and survivorship of the CRSP tape versus coins that died.

### Liu, Tsyvinski, and Wu (2022), cryptocurrency momentum

Source: Yukun Liu, Aleh Tsyvinski, Xi Wu, "Common Risk Factors in Cryptocurrency," Journal of Finance 77 (2022) 1133–1177. https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.13119. Working text quoted here: NBER Working Paper 25882, May 2019, https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w25882/w25882.pdf

What it predicts: among coins, higher recent returns predict higher next-week returns. One-, two-, three-, and four-week sorts work in their sample; eight-week and longer sorts do not, in the text below. The three-week sort is the horizon they use for the momentum factor and the one with the largest high-minus-low gap in Table 4.

Quoted rules:

> "We consider all of the coins with market capitalizations above one million dollars."

> "Each week, we sort individual cryptocurrencies into quintile portfolios based on the value of a given factor. All strategies are rebalanced weekly."

> "For the one-, two-, three-, and four-week momentum strategies, the average mean excess returns increase with the quintiles. ... The differences in the average returns of the highest and lowest quintiles are 2.7 percent for the one-week momentum, 3.3 percent for the two-week momentum, 4.1 percent for the three-week momentum, and 2.5 percent for the four-week momentum."

Table 4's means are value-weighted portfolio excess returns. The factor construction in the published paper splits coins into bottom 30 percent, middle 40 percent, and top 30 percent on three-week momentum and defines CMOM as top minus bottom. The trading strategy in section 3.2 of the working paper is the quintile sort, not the 30/40/30 factor. This bakeoff freezes the quintile strategy for three-week momentum only. The other horizons and the 30/40/30 split are not run.

Native timeframe: weekly sort, one-week hold, coins above $1 million market cap, 2014–2018 in the working paper.

4h/1h: the prediction is next week's cross-sectional return. The 4h bar is only the first completed 4h bar after the venue week closes, so the fill is not a weekly bar. There is no 4h price pattern. The cross-section is this desk's point-in-time liquid Hyperliquid perps, which is narrower than every coin above $1 million. That universe change is stated in the contract. It is not a license to add a 4h setup.

Failure modes: their sort ignores trading costs and shorting limits (they say so for the size strategy, and the same gap applies here). Value weights need a market-cap vintage dated at or before the decision. CoinGecko in this repo is live-only (`rocket/providers/coingecko.py`). Projecting today's top 100 backward is survivorship, already called out in `artifacts/futures_desk/STRATEGY_GAP.md`. Weekly boundaries in the paper are not pinned to Hyperliquid's `1w` candle. Three-week momentum includes the most recent week (`r_{3,0}`); inserting Jegadeesh-Titman's skip would be a different rule.

Contract: `cmom-r3-quintile`.

## 3. Multi-timeframe pullback / break-retest (4h location, 1h confirm)

No academic paper found in this pass states a 4h location plus a 1h confirm as the entry. The rule that does is the nave YAML, with a frozen mechanical reading on rocket PR #28 and a conflicting implementation in nave `theory_v2.py`. Those are incumbents. They are not the default winner.

### Nave `docs/technical.yaml`

Source: https://github.com/jhonnyisaacc/nave/blob/180d0b8adac03a2a999f6f9d83db45642de8f3d0/docs/technical.yaml

What it predicts: after a weekly bias and a confirming daily structure, a pullback into a 4h confluence zone, then a 1h flip inside that zone, continues toward the next confluence zone. A 1h pattern with no 4h setup is noise.

Quoted clocks and gates:

> "Direction is decided on the HTF; execution is routed through 4H and 1H."

> "Weekly bias is gated by VELOCITY, not a slow SMA. ... velocity = (close_now − close_{4 weeks ago}) / weekly_ATR_8. long if velocity > +1.2 short if velocity < −1.2 neutral otherwise (iter 14 parameter sweep selected 1.2 over 1.0/1.5/2.0 ...)."

The prose two paragraphs above that formula says the displacement must exceed 1.5 weekly ATRs. The formula line says 1.2. The 1.2 value is an outcome sweep. PR #28 froze 1.2 and refused the prose 1.5. This bakeoff does not reopen that.

> "Daily must confirm the weekly bias. If daily structure contradicts the weekly bias, stand down for the week."

> "Retracement has reached at least the 75% zone of the impulse, not the 50% alert."

> "Any backtest engine reading this section must not implement the 4H validity check as "4H trend MA matches daily trend MA". ... Implement it instead as "4H is inside a confluence zone AND a structural element has printed"."

> "A 1H candle closes above a prior 1H resistance that has flipped into support (for longs), or closes below a prior 1H support that has flipped into resistance (for shorts), inside the 4H entry zone. entry: on the close of the flip candle. sl: beyond the last 1H swing that formed before the flip."

> "1H trigger without a 4H setup is noise, not signal."

> "50% is explicitly NOT an entry."

> "The CURRENT IMPULSE LEG is the leg between the most recent confirmed daily swing low and daily swing high ... that bracket the most recent break of structure on the daily. ... Re-anchoring the impulse leg every week is the engine bug."

A conflicting line, `retroceso_75_setup`, says "After a confirmed 4H impulse." PR #28 chose the locked daily leg and left the conflict named. This bakeoff does not resolve it by looking at results.

Native timeframe: week, day, 4h, 1h, in that order. This is the family that already lives on the required clocks.

4h/1h: yes. The YAML's entry-on-the-flip-close disagrees with a next-1h-open fill. PR #28 already froze the next hourly open. The contract follows that frozen reading and labels the YAML close as the other reading, not a second contract.

Failure modes: 1.2 is outcome-selected. The YAML says it is a project refinement, not authenticated educator text (same admission in PR #28 `CONTRACT.md` and in `artifacts/futures_desk/STRATEGY_GAP.md`). Confluence factors include blocks and session lines the engines do not all compute. COT's full usage file exists in nave as `docs/cot_integration.yaml` but was missing from the PR #28 input set; COT is an optional overlay here, not the setup. Costs, spread, and funding are not in the YAML's R math.

### PR #28 mechanical reading

Source, read-only: https://github.com/jhonnyisaacc/rocket/blob/codex/crypto-strategy-research/.rocket/research/full_strategy_v1/CONTRACT.md and `engine.py` on that branch. Pull request: https://github.com/jhonnyisaacc/rocket/pull/28

`CONTRACT.md` opens: "This is a testable interpretation, NOT user-approved doctrine, a validated edge, or a complete test of all Cryptopana branches."

The chain is weekly velocity ±1.2, daily pivot structure, locked daily impulse, 75–86% band, BEN clipped to that band, confluence at least 3, 4h reaction (adverse wick at least the body, close beyond the zone midpoint), then hourly sweep, break, and retest, fill at the next hourly open, stop at the adverse hourly swing buffered 1bp, two structural objectives, reject if the first is under 1R. The 1.5× daily ATR floor is excluded. Pending setups expire eight days after admission. Details are the `pana-full` contract. That contract is an incumbent baseline, not a decision to install this chain as the live detector.

Failure modes already recorded on that branch: 0 of 6,480 snapshots admitted; historical spread, liquidity, macro, and COT were UNKNOWN; price-only results are not a full-context profitability claim. Do not loosen a blocker because the count was zero.

## 4. Derivatives microstructure, gates only

These are not standalone directional entries on a 4h/1h clock. No contract uses funding, open interest, or liquidations as the signal. A later contract may read them only as a gate if a source states the gate. None of the frozen contracts do.

### Carry and funding as their own trade

Maik Schmeling, Andreas Schrimpf, Karamfil Todorov, "Crypto Carry," SSRN 4268371, April 27, 2023. https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4268371. BIS working paper: https://www.bis.org/publ/work1087.pdf

What it predicts: the dated-futures basis (futures minus spot) is a carry. The strategy is long spot and short the dated future. High carry predicts crashes. Quoted abstract: the carry "can become very large (up to 60% p.a.)." The BIS text distinguishes this from perpetual funding and points to He et al. and Christin et al. for perpetuals.

Songrun He, Asaf Manela, Omri Ross, Victor von Wachter, "Fundamentals of Perpetual Futures," arXiv:2212.06888. The perpetual strategy in that paper is an arbitrage between spot and the perpetual when the deviation leaves the no-arbitrage bound, with longs paying shorts a funding rate when the perpetual is rich.

4h/1h: both strategies are basis trades, not a directional OHLC break. An unhedged short because funding is positive is a different bet (direction plus funding). Not contracted. `rocket/providers/hyperliquid.py` exposes a current `funding` field on `metaAndAssetCtxs` and OHLC via `fetch_candles`. It does not provide a historical funding series. Adding one would be a second data path. Not done in this phase.

### Open interest and liquidations do not yield a frozen gate

Alexander, Chen, Deng, and others, "Reconciling Open Interest with Traded Volume in Perpetual Swaps," arXiv:2310.14973. Finding used here: reported open interest on some venues is implausible or delayed. A gate that treats a single OI print as truth can be a data error.

"Where does the criticality live?," arXiv:2607.27070. Across seven BTC liquidation cascades, no state variable was an event-invariant early warning. A liquidation-flush entry rule would be invented. Not contracted.

An exchange research page that conditions a rebound on "price has stopped falling" after an OI drop is a blog confirmation story, not a frozen threshold. Omitted as a rule.

Failure modes if someone later adds a gate: funding and OI at decision time must have an availability timestamp; today's `metaAndAssetCtxs` snapshot is not the historical print; liquidation prints are not in the candle schema.

## 5. Volatility-managed / risk-scaled momentum

### Moreira and Muir (2017)

Source: Alan Moreira and Tyler Muir, "Volatility-Managed Portfolios," Journal of Finance 72 (2017) 1611–1644. https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.12513. NBER 22208: https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w22208/w22208.pdf

What it predicts: scaling an existing factor down when its recent variance was high raises the factor's Sharpe ratio, because variance moves more than expected return. It does not predict a direction by itself.

Quoted rule:

> "f^σ_{t+1} = (c / σ̂²_t(f)) f_{t+1}"

> "using the previous month realized variance as a proxy for the conditional variance"

c is chosen so the managed portfolio has the same unconditional standard deviation as the buy-and-hold factor. Realized variance is the variance of that month's daily factor returns. Native timeframe: monthly scaling of a factor that already exists.

4h/1h: there is no entry. Scaling `trb-50d` or `cmom-r3-quintile` by an inverse 4h variance would merge this paper with another family and change c. Not contracted.

Moskowitz, Ooi, and Pedersen already scale each TSMOM position by 40% / σ. That scale stays inside family 1's description. It is not a second entry, and TSMOM itself is not a 4h contract.

Failure modes: c fit to the sample's unconditional vol is in-sample by construction. Applying the scaler to a different factor, or to 4h returns, and then picking c from perp PnL, is a search.

## 6. Incumbents already specified in nave or rocket

These are historical specifications. None is the default winner. None is installed as the live `rocket crypto scan` detector by this bakeoff.

### theory-v2

Repo: https://github.com/jhonnyisaacc/nave/blob/180d0b8adac03a2a999f6f9d83db45642de8f3d0/trading/crypto/theory_v2.py

Runnable path: `TheoryV2Engine.evaluate`. Default extras (`cross_asset_fn`, `recovery_config`, `squeeze_config`) are off. `evaluate_squeeze_daily` is not on that path. `range_breakout_bias` is on that path when velocity is neutral.

The code's gates, which the contract copies and does not repair:

- Weekly: `momentum_bias`, lookback 4, threshold ±1.2, ATR window 8. Else `range_breakout_bias` (prior 7 weekly closes, range at most 1.5 ATR, break by 0.5 ATR).
- Daily: `trend` on 10 closes, deadband 0.5%. Docstring says 20. Code wins.
- Climax: a daily true range above 3× ATR-20 inside the last 10 daily bars blocks.
- Chase: last close must retrace 50% to 95% of the latest daily swing leg. If no leg is found, the gate passes.
- 4h: `trend` on 8 closes, deadband 0.5%, must match the bias. Docstring says 12. The YAML forbids this moving-average check. The incumbent code does it anyway.
- 1h: entry is the last 1h close. Stop is the wider of the last 24 1h bars' adverse extreme and 1.5× daily ATR-14. Targets are daily swings at least 1R away, else synthetic 1.5R and 2.5R. The CLI printer labels 80% and 20%. The YAML partials are 80/10/10. The signal object does not store the split.

COT: `weekly_cot_filter` blocks only when history exists and the permission is `block`. Missing history is "COT unavailable — permissive pass" (`trading/crypto/cot/cot_gate.py`). Crowded cutoff 0.85, disagreement cutoff 0.60. COT is an overlay on this incumbent, not the setup. The bakeoff contract does not require it.

Outcome taint: the YAML states that iter 14 picked 1.2 on pooled total-R. Freezing the code does not retune it. It also does not pretend the thresholds were chosen blind.

Contract: `theory-v2-code`.

## What was not merged

No family is glued to another to manufacture a signal. TSMOM is not given a 4h Donchian. Cross-sectional quintiles are not given a pullback filter. Funding is not a confirmation on `pana-full`. Vol scaling is not applied to the breakout. COT does not change a price action except where `theory-v2-code` already blocks, and only when COT history is actually supplied.
